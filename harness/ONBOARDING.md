# Bounded role onboarding

This phase establishes an independent task and its working contract. Use only your actor packet and a fresh output directory. Read `ASSIGNMENT.json`, `PACKET.json`, the common policy and your own role harness.

1. Verify the SHA-256 of every listed file before relying on it. Record failures explicitly.
2. Record your actor ID, role, domain IDs, input commit, packet hash, permitted outputs and whether any unlisted context was exposed. Do not claim operating-system isolation when only a worktree and file policy are available.
3. Write `readiness.json` and `first-pass-plan.md` in the designated outbox. Use English. Do not edit the shared checkout, settings, credentials, other tasks or repository governance.
4. Proposer plans: identify subareas and engineering decisions; define terminology, search expansions, comparison dimensions, possible failure mechanisms to investigate and meaningful experimental endpoints. Do not claim an idea is novel before searching.
5. Challenger plans: independently define nearest-work search strategies, contest-hint checks, critical baseline comparisons, saturation/utility objections and tests that could refute those objections. Do not invent objections to a proposal that does not yet exist.
6. Verifier plans: specify claim/source/locator checks, triage order, domain queue separation, escalation conditions and evidence required for a verdict. With no claims supplied, report `waiting_for_evidence`, not approval.
7. Finish this onboarding turn. Do not begin a broad web survey, expensive experiment, recurring monitoring, inter-agent conversation or self-generated follow-up. A later file packet or direct human request starts the research round.

Use no cross-task inspection or agent messaging. Do not create subordinate agents. Your final chat response is a short status and output paths; scientific content belongs in files. User-facing status may be Chinese, but all role plans and artifacts are English.
