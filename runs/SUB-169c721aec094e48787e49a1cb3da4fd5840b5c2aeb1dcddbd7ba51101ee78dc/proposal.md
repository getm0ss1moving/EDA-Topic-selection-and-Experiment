## Engineering decision and bounded scope

A timing-optimization developer evaluates several mutually exclusive, functionally equivalent cell-sizing alternatives against the same base netlist before committing one. The requested output is the same per-candidate endpoint setup-slack vector, violating-endpoint set, WNS/TNS and deterministic candidate choice as the pinned scalar evaluation of those alternatives. The optimization sequence, candidate set, physical model and tie policy are held fixed. Faster candidate scoring is useful only if total iteration time decreases without changing this decision. This is a timing-analysis subroutine contribution; candidate generation and physical optimization belong to its caller.

Initial supported domain: fixed acyclic topology, one fixed library/parasitic/constraint scenario, fixed extracted wire topology, legal same-footprint function-preserving cell replacements, NLDM cell models, ideal clocks and fixed endpoint setup semantics. Candidate-dependent pin capacitances and their effects on predecessor loads MUST be propagated; fixed wire topology does not mean fixed load. No transparent latches, SI iteration, statistical variation, CPPR, generated-clock changes, conditional-path exceptions or topology edits enter the first implementation. Unsupported contexts use independent scalar evaluation, with preparation, replay and final full-context guard checking charged. Production coverage, realistic candidate-batch sizes and benefits of this restricted slice are open questions, not assumptions of industry adoption.

S01 pp.14–15/18–19 establishes the candidate-selection/undo workflow. S03 Resizer::findResizeSlacks and journalRestore establishes an accessible contemporary integration surface, not that the present code lacks every possible cache. S12–S14 demonstrate that current contest needs extend considerably beyond this slice. General demand is supported; scoped adoption and the 20% screening threshold remain unvalidated.

## Mechanism and invariants (CLM-604)

Let candidate set C be fixed and mutually exclusive relative to base revision R. For operation q in a deterministic timing dependency DAG, define its complete input record I_q(c): operation/kernel/build identity; immutable library/table/parasitic/SDC/context versions; local candidate overlay including load and changed input caps; all ordered predecessor numerical states and timing tags read by the kernel; FP environment; tie/order information and other observable state. The task DAG includes the load-preparation, delay/slew, arrival, terminal-check and reduction dependencies. A numeric-arrival-only key is invalid.

Represent a partition P_q as pairs (M, state), where M is a nonempty subset of candidates, masks are disjoint and cover the scheduled candidate universe, and every member's complete state is identical. At q, intersect predecessor and local-overlay masks to form nonempty input classes. For each distinct complete input record, execute the ORIGINAL scalar kernel once; attach its full output to that mask. Byte-compare complete outputs before unioning masks; a hash is an index, never sufficient equality evidence. Different inputs that happen to produce the same full output may be merged after evaluation. Do not merge candidates' netlists or combine mutually exclusive edits. The operation graph, parameters and candidate-local overlays remain explicit, so a later divergence splits the relevant mask again.

Maintain sparse changed-state overlays and reuse the base for untouched operations exactly as the best scalar incremental baseline does. No credit for skipping untouched nodes already skipped by that baseline. At an endpoint, broadcast the exact scalar check result to its members; deterministic final selection and requested report expansion cost at least the needed per-candidate outputs. A batch or affected remainder exceeding the preregistered mask/memory/work cap goes through serial reference evaluation, replaying the ORIGINAL candidate from R. UNKNOWN/nonfinite/unmodeled context never becomes a certified pass. Global checkpoint change invalidates overlays and version-dependent classes.

Pseudocode specification:

    for q in topological_order(invalidated_dependency_tasks):
        input_classes = intersect_complete_dependency_partitions(q)
        output_classes = empty
        for mask, complete_input in input_classes:
            output = scalar_kernel[q](complete_input)  # unchanged operation order
            intern_with_full_equality(output_classes, mask, output)
        if charged_cap_exceeded(): reference_replay_remaining_candidates_from_R()
    expand_requested_endpoint_reports_and_choose_with_scalar_tie_policy()

This specification leaves the adapter/read-set audit as a prerequisite. Pointer identity must be normalized to stable object identity without erasing observable order/context. Hash collisions, padding bytes, signed zero, NaN payloads, version reuse, hidden mutable caches and traversal-dependent tie selection require explicit treatment. Unsupported cases fall back. Parallel kernel calls cannot silently change arithmetic or iteration order. S02 source and S03 API behavior do not prove that all read sets have been captured.

## Analytical sanity checks and strongest counter-hypothesis (CLM-606)

Executed only a tiny Python-stdlib synthetic state-propagation check, reproduced verbatim in experiment-plan.md. It is not a circuit, Liberty evaluator, OpenSTA run, wall-time benchmark or performance estimate.

For 64 synthetic lanes and 12 unary stages, four initial full states produce 768 separate scalar evaluations; a complete-key cache makes 768 lookups and 48 misses; a mask-class executor makes 48 representative transitions, plus 64 initial insertions and 64 final output materializations. Their output bytes agree. With 64 distinct states both cache misses and class transitions are 768, and mask management is extra work. Orthogonal 8-by-8 partitions produce 64 nonempty reconvergent input classes; compression can disappear. Equal arrival/slew with different tags are kept separate; +0.0 and -0.0 are distinct byte states.

Critical deduction: on the SAME deterministic task graph with complete keys and unlimited equal cache capacity, global memoization can execute each unique input record once, just like the quotient executor. The toy therefore supplies NO kernel-count advantage over the strong baseline. Only the work used to discover/represent repeated candidate contexts may differ. A baseline that stores output identities and propagates equivalent candidate masks can implement this representation too. Establishing whether that is an ordinary composition already present in the closest systems is the first stop/go question, not a detail deferred after expensive implementation.

There is also a practical adverse alternative: modern lazy incremental propagation already stops where changes cease to matter. Such stopped regions cannot be recounted as saved work. Real resizing alternatives may all produce distinct floating states through their relevant cones, while the unmodified base is already reused. Then even the favorable mask arithmetic above is irrelevant to the actual bottleneck.

## Falsifiable hypotheses, cost and headroom (CLM-607)

H1: on a documented candidate-selection workload, complete-state repetition INSIDE genuinely recomputed cones yields enough reduced dispatch/key/state-copy cost to lower end-to-end candidate scoring time against B+ (below), including read-set recording, mask joins, hashing, output expansion, memory, replay and final checks. H0: B+ is equivalent/faster; repetition occurs only in already-skipped regions; or hidden context/unique states destroys compression.

B+ = transactional incremental/lazy STA + exact full-input cache + canonical full-output interning + strongest appropriate task clustering and cache reuse, with matched task graph, information, candidate sequence, memory cap, arithmetic, core count and reporting. Also evaluate a straightforward fixed-lane vector executor and serial fresh reference. Grant B+ batching/bitsets when supported or cheaply composed; a handicap to preserve a claimed advantage is invalid.

Cost condition: saved per-candidate dispatch/key construction/copies must exceed partition intersection, representative-key construction, exact equality, allocation/GC, mask storage, load preparation, initialization, expansion and fallback costs. Count CPU work, wall time and peak RSS. K candidates still require their requested output materialization; worst-case class count is K at each operation and multi-input intersections may cause all pair tests even when few intersections survive. No asymptotic timing improvement or applicable saturation bound is claimed. S11 is evidence of an overhead problem in another execution setting, not measured headroom for this mechanism. Numerical gain and success forecasts remain null.
